Three steps, and the writing is done for you

Most documentation dies because writing it is tedious. GuideGen removes the writing.

Record

Press start and do the task exactly as you normally would. A small pill in the corner counts the steps as you go.

It writes itself

Every click becomes a step with its own screenshot, the target highlighted and numbered, and a sentence describing the action — Click "Rider Management", Type "Demo" in the "Search" field, Press Enter.

Tidy and export

Fix any wording, reorder or delete steps, hide anything sensitive, then export to the format your team actually reads.

What you get

Not a screen recorder. A document you can edit, and a guide someone can follow.

Instructions, written for you

Step text comes from the control you actually clicked — its label and its role — not a generic "click here". Fields, dropdowns, checkboxes and keystrokes each read the way a person would write them.

Screenshots that make sense

Each step is captured at the moment you clicked, with the target lifted out and numbered. Guides crop to the part of the page that matters instead of shrinking the whole window until nobody can read it.

Redaction that actually redacts

Drag a box over anything sensitive and it is pixelated in every export, permanently. Password fields are masked the moment they are captured.

Narration without a cloud

The video export can speak each step aloud in a natural voice. The speech is synthesized on your own computer, so the text of your internal processes is never sent to a text-to-speech service.

Nothing leaves your machine

No account to record. No server involved. Your screenshots are stored in your own browser and are never uploaded — unless you deliberately publish a guide to get a shareable link.

Built for the screens you can't put in the cloud

The processes most worth documenting tend to live in admin panels full of real customer data. That is exactly the content people are told not to upload to a third-party tool.

GuideGen records locally and exports locally. Recording needs no account, every export format is generated inside your browser, and even the voiceover is produced on your own machine. If you never publish a guide, we never receive anything — there is nowhere for it to go.

Publishing a guide for a shareable link is a separate, deliberate action, and only that guide is uploaded. Full detail in the privacy policy.

Questions worth asking first

Does it work outside the browser?

No. GuideGen documents web applications — anything you can open in a Chromium browser. It cannot capture native desktop software.

Can I share a guide as a link?

Shareable links are the feature being built right now. Today you export a self-contained HTML file — a single file with the images embedded — which you can drop into Google Drive, Notion, SharePoint or an email. Join the list above and I'll tell you when links arrive.

What languages does it handle?

Step descriptions are generated in English at the moment. It will happily record an interface in any language, but the sentences it writes around it are English.

Why does it ask for access to all websites?

Because it cannot know in advance which application you'll want to document — it might be any internal dashboard. The recorder does nothing until you press start, and it reads only the label and position of the element you click.

Is it really free?

Yes, and with no card required. Paid plans may appear later for team features; if that happens, people already using it get notice and a choice rather than a surprise bill.

How big is the install?

Around 68 MB, which is unusual for an extension. Almost all of it is the neural voice model that lets narration run offline instead of sending your text to a server.
